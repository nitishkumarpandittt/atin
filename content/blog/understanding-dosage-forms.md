Our catalogue lists more than twenty dosage forms and formats. Here is a plain-language overview of what each term means as it appears in product listings. This is general trade information, not guidance on the use of any medicine.

## Solid forms

- **Tablet**: a compressed solid unit. Listings may add letters such as *DT* (dispersible tablet) or *SR* (sustained release) to the brand or composition.
- **Capsule**: a hard shell containing powder or granules.
- **Softgel capsule**: a soft, sealed gelatin shell, often containing an oil-based fill.

## Liquid forms

- **Syrup**: a ready-to-use liquid.
- **Suspension**: a liquid in which particles are dispersed; packs usually advise shaking before use.
- **Dry syrup**: a powder supplied in a bottle, to be reconstituted with water as directed on the pack.
- **Oral drops**: a concentrated liquid supplied with a dropper.

## Injectables

Supplied as vials, ampoules or pre-filled packs. Pack descriptions such as *vial packing* or *dispo pack* describe the presentation.

## Topical and other formats

- **Ointment, gel, cream and lotion**: preparations for external application, differing in base and texture.
- **Soap, shampoo and dusting powder**: formats within the derma and personal care segments.
- **Eye, ear and nasal drops or sprays**: supplied in small dropper bottles or metered-dose sprays.
- **Sachet and powder**: single-dose sachets or jars, used in the nutrition segment.

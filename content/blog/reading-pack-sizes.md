A trade catalogue lists the same few pack formats again and again. Knowing how to read them saves time when you compare products and place orders.

## Strips and boxes

A pack written as **10 x 10** usually means ten strips in a box, with ten units in each strip, so one box holds 100 units. **10 x 1 x 10** adds a middle layer: ten strips, each packed individually, inside a box of ten.

Some listings show smaller counts such as **10 x 6** or **10 x 3**, where each strip holds fewer units.

## Alu-Alu and blister

The note in brackets describes the strip material:

- **Alu-Alu** strips are aluminium on both sides.
- **Blister** strips have a clear or coloured formed layer over a foil backing.
- **Poly strip** refers to a flexible laminated strip.

Where a listing shows both, for example *Alu-Alu / blister*, the product is available in either format.

## Liquids, tubes and jars

For syrups, suspensions and drops the pack is given by volume, such as **60 ml with carton** or **30 ml with dropper**. Topicals and powders are listed by weight, such as **15 gm with carton** or **200 gm in plastic jar**.

## Why it matters

Pack format affects storage space, shelf display and how you count stock. When you send us an enquiry, including the brand name and the pack you need helps us respond faster and more accurately.
